**Figure 4 | SFT confidence is calibrated within field, and selective prediction concentrates accuracy in high-confidence subsets.**

**a**, Mean predicted probability assigned to the chosen label, separated by whether the prediction was correct (filled dark-blue circle) or incorrect (open dark-blue circle), for each of the eight social-science fields. Markers are 3-SFT-model means (Qwen3-30B SFT, Qwen3-4B SFT, GPT-4.1-nano SFT). Per-field benchmark sizes are *n* = 200 articles for the seven non-management fields and *n* = 120 articles for management. Faint jittered dots show the per-model values. The blue arrow connects incorrect to correct means; its length is the **confidence gap** = mean *P*(correct) − mean *P*(wrong). Fields are ranked by gap, descending. The gap is positive in every field, indicating that the SFT models assign higher probability when they are right than when they are wrong.

**b**, Selective-prediction curves for Qwen3-30B SFT, shown as a representative SFT architecture. Each line traces prediction accuracy on the most-confident *k*% of articles per field, for *k* ∈ {10, 15, 20, 25, 33, 50, 100}. All eight fields are plotted explicitly: line color matches the field legend at the right edge of the panel, with line-end labels replacing a separate legend. The black dashed curve is the mean GPT-5.5 chat/log-probability comparator across the same eight field benchmarks, and the grey band is its per-field min-max range at each coverage threshold. GPT-5.5 is plotted here because its chat/log-probability run supplies token probabilities for confidence sorting, whereas the sampled frontier-reasoning cohort produced sampled labels. Reference lines mark 80 % accuracy (dotted) and chance (25 %, dashed). In six of eight fields, Qwen3-30B accuracy on the top-25 % most-confident articles exceeds 80 %.

**c**, Across the eight fields, mean confidence gap (3-SFT-model mean of mean *P*(correct) − mean *P*(wrong); *x*-axis) and mean prediction accuracy (*y*-axis, predicted tier = ground-truth tier) rise together descriptively (Spearman ρ = +0.33, *p* = 0.42, *n* = 8 fields). The dashed line is a visual reference, not a model fit; we do not interpret this trend as causal — fields where the model is confidently correct are also fields where it is more often correct, but the panel is descriptive.

**d**, Triage operating point: for each field, prediction accuracy at full coverage (light blue) versus accuracy on the top-25 % most-confident articles (dark blue). Full coverage = 200 articles per field for the seven non-management fields, 120 for management; top-25 % = 50 most-confident articles per non-management field, 30 for management. All values are 3-SFT-model means. Numbers above the bar pairs are the lift in percentage points (pp = percentage points), ranked descending. Lift is positive in every field, ranging from +13 pp (Pol. Science) to +36 pp (Sociology). The chance baseline (25 %) is shown for reference.

Prediction accuracy is the proportion of articles for which the predicted tier matches the ground-truth tier. Field abbreviations: Psych = Psychology (Multidisciplinary), Sociol = Sociology, Econ = Economics, PubAd = Public Administration, Comm = Communication, BusFin = Business & Finance, PolSci = Political Science, Mgmt = Management. Data source: rating-level outputs of three SFT models on per-field benchmarks (200 articles for each of seven non-management fields, 120 for management; eight fields × three models = 4,560 ratings total). Field colors in panels b and c follow the figure-style guide; panels a and d use a light/dark blue contrast to emphasize the coverage manipulation rather than the field, since the field identity is given by the *x*-axis tick labels.
